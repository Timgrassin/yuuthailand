# Staylong competitor research: long-stay Thailand visa content

Research date: 4 November 2026. Method: ~35 WebSearch queries (extended mode, US-based search index) covering the query set in the brief plus follow-ups. **WebFetch was blocked in this environment** (DNS failure for every non-allowlisted host, e.g. thai-visa-services.com, stampstay.com), so everything below comes from search result titles and snippets. "Appears for" means the domain showed up in the result set for that query, not a measured Google rank position. No traffic, backlink or AI-citation data was available (Ahrefs/Similarweb MCPs failed to connect). Items marked **[unverified]** come from a single non-official source or could not be confirmed.

---

## 1. Landscape summary

1. The SERP is crowded and almost entirely commercial: visa agencies, "AI visa platforms", law-firm blogs, property agents and gyms/schools all publish "2026 guide" pages. Official sites almost never surface for head terms, except ltr.boi.go.th for LTR and embassy pages for DTV-by-country.
2. Two fast-moving 2026 events are reshaping demand. First, the DTV rule change on 31 Aug 2026: you must apply in your country of nationality or residence, and a criminal record certificate is now required. Second, the visa-exemption cut from 60 to 30 days on 15 Sep 2026. A third, smaller one: Thailand Privilege Bronze kept getting extended (31 Mar, then 30 Sep, then 31 Dec 2026). Every site rewrote its pages, and the details conflict: police-cert validity, seasoning months, per-person or per-family funds, how many countries are affected.
3. The strongest content operators are **Issa Compass** (VC-backed "AI visa platform" with a huge /insights hub), **thai-visa-services.com** (anonymous "editorial team" with agency reviews, a news tracker, checklists and a DTV eligibility tree), **thaiembassy.com / siam-legal.com** (Siam Legal's two-domain play), **thethaiger.com** (news plus a visa hub), and **thailawonline.com** (glossary-style, very broad).
4. Nobody combines dated rules, official-source citations and a vetted provider directory. Rule pages cite agencies, and directories (thaivisareviews.com, NOW Muay Thai's "Verified DTV-Ready", Muay Thai Time) don't explain rules.
5. Trust is the open wound. There was a fraud raid on a Sai Mai agency in Aug 2026, with about 400 clients exposed **[single outlet]**. Fake TDAC lookalike sites have spread, Thai Visa Centre is contested, and thaiembassy.com is a private site that looks official. Searchers explicitly ask "is X legit?".
6. Tools exist but are shallow. Stampstay, thainiverse, thailandvisacentre.com, skipborders and thai-visa-services all run quizzes, and all of them are lead funnels for their own service. None shows rule versions, effective dates or sources.
7. There is no public, maintained list of MOE/OPEC-licensed Thai language schools, and no independent price comparison. ED-school SERPs are school marketing pages plus Q&A scrapes (ask.in.th).
8. Muay Thai DTV is a busy niche dominated by gyms' own blogs. The rules people ask about are contested: which gyms can issue letters (Sports Authority of Thailand registration?), the minimum course length (6, 9 or 12 months), and whether attendance is tracked.
9. Forums (ASEAN NOW, Thai Visa Advice FB group, Reddit) remain the de-facto "what's happening at my office right now" layer. Their threads are inconsistent: some posters say you must keep 800k all year, others say a 400k floor. They are also frequently scraped by Q&A farms.
10. The opportunity is to become the citable primary-ish source. That means effective dates, change logs, per-embassy and per-office variance, and verified providers. AI answers currently stitch together conflicting agency snippets and hedge with "check the official site".

## 3. Per-competitor notes

**Issa Compass.** The biggest threat for AI-search and long-tail queries. It ranks for almost every query tested, including niche ones: per-embassy DTV rejections, Muay Thai soft-power stays, professional athletes and soft power, and spouse/dependants. It publishes press releases claiming 100k users and $1M ARR, backed by 500 Global, Iterative and Sequoia Sprout. Its "AI verification engine" is a document checker. Weaknesses: it is a sales funnel ("5 best visa services… and how Issa stands out"), cites self-reported approval stats, and does not show official-source citations or effective dates in snippets. Its disclaimer says it is not a law firm. It has no provider-vetting role, and it is itself a provider.

**thai-visa-services.com.** The closest overall competitor. It has agency reviews (including takedowns of TVC and of thaiembassy.com), a "visa news tracker", a DTV eligibility decision tree, document checklists, immigration-office guides (Chiang Mai with queue tips) and comparison pages (Elite vs LTR, Non-O vs O-A, best long-stay visa). The anonymous "Editorial Team" byline is the obvious weakness for a site that sells trust. I could not see whether the news tracker links to primary sources or offers RSS (fetch blocked).

**thaiembassy.com + siam-legal.com.** Siam Legal holds two SERP slots for many queries. thaiembassy.com's name implies officialness, competitors attack it for that, and it is a reputational liability. The content is long and broad, with comments answered by staff. Some pages (TM30) are years old even when titled 2026.

**The Thaiger.** A media authority with a /visas/ hub (DTV, retirement Non-O/O-A/O-X) and guides like "Why some trainees still choose the ED over the DTV" (good angle). It reports news quickly but mixes in unconfirmed items (Cabinet approval in May). There are no tools and no provider data.

**thailawonline.com.** Wide glossary coverage (TM30, DTV, retirement, marriage, ED "90 days / 1 year / ED Plus"). It ranks on breadth. A weak spot, though an AI-citation-friendly one.

**Law firms (Lexbangkok, Benoit & Partners, Integrity Legal).** Lexbangkok is fresh on 2026 changes. Benoit leans corporate. Integrity Legal is dormant. None has tools.

**Thai Visa Centre (tvc.co.th).** A large agency with self-hosted reviews (4,608) and lifestyle and comparison guides. Its reputation is contested. This is a reason for Staylong never to rely on self-hosted review counts.

**thaivisareviews.com.** A direct analogue to Staylong's directory but agencies only. It claims a business-registration check plus a fake-review filter, and gives "Agency of the Month" awards. It is young (Oct 2025). It does not cover schools or gyms, and has no visa rules or a last-checked date per field.

**Quiz/tool sites (stampstay, thainiverse, thailandvisacentre.com, skipborders, nomadsembassy).** They validate the demand for a checker. Thainiverse makes rule claims that can't be confirmed ("Thai language no longer soft power"). None states which rule version produced the answer.

**Muay Thai platforms (NOW Muay Thai, Muay Thai Time) and gym blogs.** NOW Muay Thai's "Verified DTV-Ready" list is the most direct competitor to Staylong's camp directory. Its verification method isn't visible in snippets. Muay Thai Time has data depth (301 camps, 62 with visa support, 32 naming DTV) but relies on self-declared visa support. The gym blogs contradict each other on the 6, 9 or 12-month minimum and on whether attendance is tracked.

**Language schools.** Prices cluster around 36k-49k THB for 8-14 months in Bangkok. Examples: Bangkok Language School at 47,500 for 14 months, GEOS at 49,000 for 14 months, Liberty at 48,000. Immigration fees are often excluded. The "approval rates" are self-reported. Duke is forum-favoured for real learning ("don't pick Duke if you just want the visa"). Schools have not published an MOE/OPEC licence list. Enforcement is rising: about 10,000 ED visas were reported cancelled from May 2025, and 668 were revoked Jan-May 2026 after attendance checks **[secondary sources]**.

**DTV niche/EMD sites.** These are numerous, templated and contradictory. dtv.in.th has per-country pages (/country/united-states), which proves the visa x country pattern gets traction.

**Elite agents.** They win on freshness for Bronze deadline news but contradict each other. The Bronze deadline history is a perfect demonstration of what a dated change log does better.

**Official sources.** ltr.boi.go.th ranks for LTR, and embassy pages rank for per-country DTV (London, LA, The Hague). thaievisa.go.th is the mandatory portal, but its requirements vary by mission, its checklists conflict, and lookalike sites (thaievisa.org) confuse people. It also carries Trustpilot complaints. The tm47 and tm30 portals are reported slow or down. Thailand Privilege's official site did not surface for the price query, so agents own that SERP.

**Forums.** ASEAN NOW is still active and ranks for "how do I get a retirement visa" style queries. The Thai Visa Advice FB group closes comments after one answer and pushes DIY. ask.in.th / asq.in.th scrape these into Q&A pages that rank for long-tail queries. Dominant questions: 800k seasoning (2 vs 3 months; 400k floor vs 800k all year), income letters after embassies stopped issuing them, which office handles what, DTV rejections, gym and school legitimacy, and whether retirees over 50 can use the DTV.

AI-search angles. Assistants hedge or get these wrong because the web disagrees:
- "Is the Thai visa exemption 30 or 60 days now?" (effective 15 Sep 2026; which countries; count varies 54/60/93 across outlets).
- "When does Thailand Privilege Bronze end?" (31 Mar, then 30 Sep, then 31 Dec 2026).
- "Does the DTV need a criminal record check / can I apply from Laos?"
- "How long must 800k be in the bank for a retirement extension?"
- "Can I study Thai on a DTV?" (thainiverse claims it changed; unconfirmed).
- "Minimum Muay Thai course length for DTV?" (6, 9 or 12 months).
- "Do DTV dependants each need 500k?"

Pages that answer these in one sourced sentence, with an effective date, schema `dateModified` and a "sources" block, are the likeliest to be cited.
