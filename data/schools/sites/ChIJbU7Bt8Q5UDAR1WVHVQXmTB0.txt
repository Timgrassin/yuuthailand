 Frequently Asked 
 Common Questions
 Do I need a lawyer for a Thai retirement visa? + 
 It isn't legally required, but the retirement (non-immigrant O-A/O-X) visa involves specific financial and documentation requirements that must be met precisely and renewed annually. Legal guidance reduces the risk of a rejected application or a lapse in status.
 Can foreigners own property in Thailand? + 
 Foreigners generally cannot own land directly, but can own condominium units within the foreign ownership quota, and can structure other property interests through long-term leases or approved company structures. The right structure depends on the property and your circumstances.
 What's the difference between a marriage visa and a retirement visa? + 
 A marriage (non-immigrant O) visa is based on marriage to a Thai national and has its own income and documentation requirements; a retirement visa is based on age (50+) and financial criteria and carries no marriage requirement. Each has different renewal and reporting obligations.
 What is 90-day reporting and a re-entry permit? + 
 Long-stay visa holders must report their address to immigration every 90 days. A re-entry permit preserves your visa status if you leave and re-enter Thailand before your next renewal — without one, leaving the country can void your visa.
 Can I set up a company in Thailand as a foreigner? + 
 Yes, through a Thai limited company, BOI-promoted investment, or a Foreign Business License, depending on your industry and ownership structure. Certain sectors carry foreign ownership restrictions, so the right structure should be confirmed before you register.
 How does a consultation with Marchalaw work? + 
 Consultations are available in person in Phuket or remotely, in English or Thai. Bring whatever documents you have — even incomplete ones — and you'll leave with a clear picture of what's required and what it will cost.
