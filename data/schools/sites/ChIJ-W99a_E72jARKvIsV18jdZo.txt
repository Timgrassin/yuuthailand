 Common Questions 
 Thailand Visa FAQ
 Quick answers to the questions we hear most often.
 What visa options are available for retirees in Thailand?
 + 
 Most retirees use either a Non-O or Non-O-A retirement route. The right choice depends on where you apply, your financial situation, and your timeline. Applicants are generally required to be 50 or over. See our Retirement Visa Thailand page for more detail.
 How do I extend my visa in Chiang Mai or Bangkok?
 + 
 Extensions are handled at the local immigration office. Requirements vary by visa type, but generally include documents, financial evidence, address paperwork, and filing before your current permission expires. We help clients understand what is needed before they attend.
 Do I need to do 90-day reporting on a long-stay visa?
 + 
 Yes. 90-day address reporting applies to most long-stay foreigners in Thailand and is a separate obligation from your visa renewal. Missing it can result in a fine. See our 90-Day Report Thailand page for more.
 What is a work permit and who needs one in Thailand?
 + 
 If you work, run a business, or earn income in Thailand, you generally need a valid work permit alongside the correct visa. The two are linked — your visa category must support work permit eligibility. See our Work Permit Thailand page for more.
 How do I get in touch with Visa Support Thailand?
 + 
 You can reach us by phone at 064-289-8974 , by email at info@visasupportthailand.com , or via LINE (contact Orange). The free Visa Assistant is also available for initial guidance.
 Do I need a re-entry permit before leaving Thailand on a long-stay visa?
 + 
 Yes — if you leave Thailand without a re-entry permit while on a permission to stay, you lose your current visa status and must start the process again from outside the country. A re-entry permit (single or multiple) must be obtained at immigration before you depart. We can handle this for you as part of our standard service. See our Re-Entry Permit Thailand page for full details.
 Can I get a marriage visa if I am married to a Thai national?
 + 
 Yes. A Non-O visa based on marriage is available to foreign nationals legally married to a Thai citizen. The annual extension requires proof of marriage, financial evidence (either 400,000 THB seasoned in a Thai bank account or a monthly income of 40,000 THB), and a home visit from immigration officers. See our Marriage Visa Thailand page for the full requirements.
 What is the DTV (Destination Thailand Visa) and who is it for?
 + 
 The DTV is a 5-year, multiple-entry visa introduced in 2024 for remote workers, freelancers, and long-stay visitors. Applicants must demonstrate at least 500,000 THB in available funds. Each entry allows a 180-day stay and it does not automatically include a work permit. See our DTV Visa page for requirements and how to apply.
 What is the Thailand Privilege Visa and how does it work?
 + 
 The Thailand Privilege Visa (formerly Elite Visa) is a paid long-term residency program offering 5 to 20 years of stay depending on the package chosen. It suits those who prefer a simple, low-maintenance route to long-term residence without annual immigration appointments. See our Thailand Privilege Visa page for current packages and costs.
 Can I register a company in Thailand as a foreigner?
 + 
 Yes, with conditions. Most foreign-owned businesses in Thailand use a Thai Limited Company structure, which typically requires Thai shareholders to hold at least 51% of shares unless the business operates under a Foreign Business Licence. Registering a company also opens the path to a Non-B business visa and a work permit. We guide you from setup through to full work authorisation. See our Company Registration Thailand page for more.
