 FAQ
 What is an employer of record (EOR), and how does it work? 
 An EOR is a third party that enables you to hire employees in a location without needing to establish a legal entity in that location. The EOR takes on the legal responsibilities of being the employer for your workforce, including payroll, taxes, and compliance. They manage your HR processes end to end by setting up the employment contracts, paying your employees on your behalf, managing the local legal compliance paperwork, and supporting your employees locally on the ground when they need it. 
 What are the benefits of using an EOR instead of hiring directly? 
 With an EOR, you are able to hire workers in another country without the costly burden of setting up a legal entity in that country and satisfying its compliance and administrative obligations. The EOR takes on these responsibilities, which include registering employees in the local tax, social security, and healthcare schemes. 
 What are the benefits of hiring overseas staff directly instead of using an EOR? 
 Hiring overseas staff directly can lead to long-term cost efficiency, as establishing a foreign entity can end up being more economical in the long run compared to the ongoing fees associated with using an EOR. 
 What is the difference between an EOR and a PEO (professional employer organization)? 
 An EOR allows a company to hire employees in a different location without needing to establish a legal entity in that location. The EOR takes on the legal responsibilities of being the local employer, including payroll, taxes, and compliance. 
 A PEO, on the other hand, typically provides outsourced HR services for organizations that already have a legal entity in that location. The PEO helps manage employee benefits, payroll, and compliance but does not employ the workers directly. 
 While they can complement each other, they serve different purposes and can operate independently based on an organization&#8217;s needs: 
 EOR without PEO: If a company needs to hire employees in another country without setting up a local entity, but they are capable of managing their HR administration remotely, they can use EOR services alone. 
 PEO without EOR: If a company already has a legal entity and needs help with HR administration, they can engage a PEO without needing EOR services.
