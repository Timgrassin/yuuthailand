 The Johnstone Clan
 The Johnstone family name is part of a Scottish clan that goes far back to 1174 when the first known person of this name was John Johnston, who gave his name to the lands of Annandale in Dumfrieshire.
 His Great Grandson, named Sir John Johnstone, was a Knight of the county of Dumfries who signed the Ragman Roll of King Edward I of England in 1296.
 Over the centuries, the Johnstone clan were involved in many battles against the English for Sottish independence. Since these conflicts, the family grew in more peaceful times, having travelled far and wide.
 Today we fight our client battles in the courthouses of Pattaya and Bangkok using the sharpest minds in the legal services industry.

 Civil Law 
 People often get confused between criminal law and civil law, and indeed, sometimes, the boundaries can become blurred. The primary purpose of criminal law is to punish a crime, whereas, with civil law, the goal is to find a remedy or solution for the injured party, such as compensation. The objective of civil law is to maintain stability and prevent people from taking matters into their own hands. Civil law cases are brought by private parties such as an individual or company instead of the police or government body.
 At Johnstone Law Services, we have a dedicated team to handle civil lawsuits. They will represent you in court or during arbitration meetings. Often negotiations are held before that case comes to court to find an amicable solution. However, where this is not possible, we will represent you in court, fighting your case to ascertain liability. We will, of course, advise you when we believe it is not worth pursuing or defending a claim and what settlement you should offer or accept.
 It is important to remember that our legal teams are acting on your behalf and will fight to achieve the best possible settlement. Any judgement made in a civil court can be appealed by either party, and this is again something that we can assist you with.
 Should you require more information about civil law and how we can represent you, please contact us.

 Marriage 
 If you are planning to get married, you must make sure that you have everything prepared beforehand. In Thailand, like anywhere else, you and your partner must be legally allowed to get married, meaning that any divorce proceedings have been completed. Of course, this will need to be proven, and for a foreign national, you will need to acquire the correct paperwork from your home country and your embassy. Preparing the paperwork ready for the marriage license can be complex, and this is where Johnstone Law Firm can help. For Thais, this can be obtained from their Amphur in their hometown.
 Once you have the documentation, you can be married, with most of the legal formalities either taking place at the end of the ceremony or later at the Amphur. Once you have the Thai documentation and the marriage has been registered, you can then begin getting the marriage recognised in your home country. Again, this is quite complicated and something that we can assist you with.
 If you want more information about our marriage services, please contact us.
 Prenuptial Agreement 
 Unfortunately, with a high percentage of marriages ending in divorce, some couples opt to prepare a prenuptial agreement, often when there is a disparity in the ownership of assets, or a particular asset is an “heirloom”. A prenuptial agreement often referred to as a prenup, is a document that is signed before a couple gets married or enters a civil partnership. The agreement clearly outlines the ownership of the couple’s belongings and how they should be split should they separate.
 Although prenuptial agreements are not legally binding in some countries such as the UK, most courts consider them, and they provide significant protection. If you would like more information about prenuptial agreements, please contact us.

 Bail Bond 
 A bail bond is a form of surety bond and is an agreement established, usually by a court, whereby a sum of money is “put up” to guarantee that a criminal defendant will appear for trial. The sum is only payable if the bail terms are breached. The bail bond will be co-signed by a bondsman, who will charge the defendant a fee for acting as the guarantor that the bond will be paid. Bail figures vary significantly and depend on the severity of the crime and the potential risk of the defendant breaching the terms of their bail.
 At Johnstone Law Firm, we can assist you with obtaining a bail bond. If you would like more information about this service, please contact us.
