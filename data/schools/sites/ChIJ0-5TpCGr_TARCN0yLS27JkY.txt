 // QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS 
 General FAQ
 Which type of visa should I apply for? 
 It depends on your purpose of stay in Thailand. Common visa categories include:
• Tourist Visa – for short trips for leisure.
• Non-Immigrant Visa “B” – for business or employment.
• Non-Immigrant Visa “ED” – for studying.
• Non-Immigrant Visa “O/A” or “O” – for retirement.
• Spouse/Family Visa – if you’re married to a Thai national.
A consultant will help assess your situation (plans, length of stay, nationality, etc.) and recommend the best category. 
 What documents do I need to apply for a visa? 
 While required documents vary by visa type and nationality, the core items usually include:
• Valid passport with at least 6 months remaining.
• Completed visa application form.
• Passport-style photos meeting specifications.
• Proof of financial means (bank statements).
• Additional documents depending on visa type (e.g., employment letter, acceptance letter from a school, marriage certificate). 
 Can I apply for or change a visa while I’m already in Thailand? 
 Some visas can be extended or changed inside Thailand, such as tourist visa extensions. But most initial long-stay or entry visas (like work visas or spouse visas) must be applied for at a Thai embassy or consulate abroad before arrival. Extensions and status changes within Thailand are possible but require you to meet specific immigration office rules. 
 How long does the visa application process take? 
 Processing times vary by embassy/consulate and visa type. Typical timelines are:
• 3‒10 business days for many typical visas via embassies.
• Same-day processing is possible for visa extensions inside Thailand.
• Consultants can often submit applications on your behalf and help track updates to speed things up.
 What happens if my visa is denied? 
 If a visa is denied, you usually get a reason for refusal. Options include:
• Re-apply with corrected or additional documents.
• Seek appeal through the relevant embassy/immigration office, if available.
• Consultancies often help review your application before submission to reduce the risk of refusal and advise on next steps if a denial occurs.
