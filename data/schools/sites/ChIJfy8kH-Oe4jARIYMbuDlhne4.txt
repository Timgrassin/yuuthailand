 Frequently Asked Questions 
 What is the legal basis of Arbitration in Thailand? 
 Arbitration in Thailand is governed by the Arbitration Act B.E. 2545 (2002), which aligns closely with the UNCITRAL Model Law. The Act allows disputes to be resolved privately under Thai or international arbitration rules, with awards recognized and enforceable by Thai courts. 
 How do I refer a dispute for Arbitration? 
 A dispute can be referred to arbitration when both parties have agreed to an arbitration clause in their contract or sign an agreement to arbitrate after the dispute arises. Once the clause is triggered, a formal request is submitted to the chosen arbitration center, and the proceedings begin according to that institution’s rules. 
 Where in Thailand are Arbitration disputes usually filed? 
 Arbitration disputes in Thailand are typically filed with the Thai Arbitration Institute (TAI) or the Thailand Arbitration Center (THAC). The correct venue depends on the contract’s arbitration clause or the parties’ joint agreement. 
 Are interim remedies available during Arbitration Proceedings? 
 Yes. Parties may request interim relief from the arbitral tribunal or apply directly to the Thai courts for urgent measures such as injunctions, asset preservation orders, or directives to prevent further harm while the arbitration is ongoing. 
 How do I enforce an Arbitration Award? 
 Arbitral awards can be enforced through the Thai courts. After submitting a petition for recognition and enforcement, the court reviews the award under Thailand’s Arbitration Act and, if approved, the award becomes legally binding and enforceable like a court judgment.
